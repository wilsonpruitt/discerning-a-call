## What changed (and what didn't)

**The 1996 hinge — the deacon's meaning flips:**
- **Pre-1996 (1977 / 1985 / 1988):** there are **two ordinations**. Everyone is **ordained deacon first** (as a *probationary* or *associate* member), then later **ordained elder** at full connection. The deacon is a **transitional** step, not a destination. Associate members are also "ordained deacon."
- **Post-1996 (2009 / 2013):** there is **one ordination**. A candidate is **commissioned** (not ordained) as a **provisional member**, then **ordained once** — as **deacon *or* elder** — at full membership. Deacon and elder are now **parallel permanent orders** (Order of Deacon / Order of Elder), reached on separate but equal tracks.

**Vocabulary shifts:**
- "Probationary member" → **"provisional member"** (+ the new act of **commissioning**).
- **"Supervising pastor"** (1985/1988) → **"candidacy mentor"** (2013). Both editions literally gloss the role as *"mentor or facilitator."*  ← directly relevant to the mentor-training module.
- **Associate membership** demoted from a live track (prominent, 1977–88) to an explicit **legacy/optional** status (2009/2013).

**Tone / format drift:**
- 1977 = pastoral prose → 1985/1988 = bureaucratic 10-step checklist (peak proceduralism) → 2009 = trimmed 8 steps → **2013 = back to warm narrative groupings** (CEB, "whether you feel called to serve as a local pastor, a deacon or an elder…"). The 2013 edition also introduces the **online candidacy application system**.

**The constants (all five editions):**
- Step 1 is *always*: **read *The Christian as Minister*** and **talk to your pastor**.
- Candidacy always runs **local church (PPRC/SPRC + Charge Conference) → District Superintendent → district Committee on Ordained Ministry → certification.**
- A **mentor figure** accompanies the candidate throughout (title changes; function constant).
- Candidacy, once certified, is **renewed annually** (from 1985 on).
- The **local-pastor license is always optional / parallel**, never a required rung.
